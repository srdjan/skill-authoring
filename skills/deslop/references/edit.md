# Edit Playbook

## Objective

Remove AI writing patterns with the minimum effective edit. Make the prose
sound like the same writer, only sharper and clearer.

## Preserve Voice

Deslopping removes AI patterns. It does not flatten a specific human into
generic-clean. Note three to five voice signals before editing. These can
include vocabulary, cadence, bluntness, humor, admitted uncertainty, or
digressions.

Keep strong human sentences untouched. Preserve profanity, self-interruptions,
honest admissions, and unusual sentence shapes when they belong to the writer.
The cut rules below are conditional, not absolute. When unsure whether a line
is slop or voice, leave it.

## Core Rules

### 1. Cut filler phrases

Remove throat-clearing, emphasis crutches, business jargon, and
meta-commentary. Use [phrases.md](phrases.md) for the full catalog.

### 2. Break formulaic structures

Rewrite repeated binary contrasts, negative listings, dramatic fragments,
self-posed questions, and anaphora or tricolon abuse. Use
[structures.md](structures.md) for patterns and fixes.

### 3. Eliminate AI tropes

Remove empty magic adverbs, the "serves as" dodge, false ranges, superficial
participle analysis, invented labels, inflated stakes, and false vulnerability.
Use [tropes.md](tropes.md) for the full catalog.

### 4. Use active voice with human subjects

Name the actor. Use `we` for the author's scientific work and `you` for direct
blog prose when that register fits.

### 5. Be specific

Replace vague declarations, unsupported extremes, and unnamed authorities with
the concrete fact. Keep precise domain terminology.

Mannered prose substitutes metaphor and flourish for direct statements. Replace
"a dial worth turning" with "a parameter worth varying." Replace "this point
earns its keep" with "this point still matters." Such phrases display the
writer instead of conveying the idea. They make readers work harder and can
introduce meanings that the writer did not intend. When a literal phrase is
available, use it.

### 6. Match register to context

Keep scientific prose formal and source-specific. Make blogs and newsletters
direct without forcing a casual voice onto the writer.

### 7. Vary rhythm

Mix sentence lengths and paragraph endings. Do not stack fragments for false
emphasis. Use a list only when the content is a list. Do not use em dashes.

### 8. Trust readers

State facts directly. Remove softening, hand-holding, pedagogical filler, and
fractal summaries when the audience does not need them.

### 9. Watch formatting tells

Remove bold-first bullet patterns, decorative Unicode, em dashes, and
signposted conclusions. Preserve formatting that serves the document.

### 10. Do not dilute

State each point once and support it. Cut repeated arguments, stacked analogies,
and a metaphor that continues after it has done its work.

## Quick Checks

Each check raises a flag. It is not an automatic delete.

- Cut an adverb only when it adds no meaning.
- Replace passive voice only when a useful actor exists.
- Remove throat-clearing and immediate rhetorical answers.
- Vary repeated sentence lengths and punch-line endings.
- Replace vague declarations with the specific implication.
- Remove repeated metaphors and filler transitions.
- Use one or two items when a prose tricolon is formulaic.

For an internal quality check, rate directness, rhythm, trust, authenticity, and
density from 1 to 10. Below 35/50: revise. Do not include the score unless the
user requests it.

Use [examples.md](examples.md) when a pattern-specific transformation helps.

## Output Contract

Deliver the complete revised text. Add a concise `What changed` note that names
the patterns removed. Do not add a generic preface or score.

## Verification

Compare the draft and revision. Confirm that facts, citations, uncertainty,
register, and intended meaning remain intact. Recheck the protected voice
signals and restore any line whose personality was lost without a clarity gain.
